I am a **herpetologist and conservation biologist** based in South Korea. My research investigates how amphibians and reptiles respond, adapt, and survive in rapidly changing environments—focusing on urbanization, biological invasions, climate shifts, and anthropogenic disturbances.

By combining extensive field surveys, ecological modeling, and policy analysis, I aim to provide evidence-based strategies for wildlife management and biodiversity conservation.

---

## 🔬 Core Research Focus

* **Invasive Species & Urban Biosecurity:** Tracking non-native reptiles and amphibians—such as the established population of the Peking gecko (*Gekko swinhonis*) in Incheon—to clarify introduction pathways, ecological impacts, and urban management strategies.
* **Amphibian Ecology & Stress Adaptation:** Examining physiological, developmental, and behavioral responses of native Korean species (including *Dryophytes japonicus*, *Dryophytes suweonensis*, and *Bufo gargarizans*) to environmental stressors such as salinity and microclimate shifts.
* **Habitat Ecology & Conservation Policy:** Evaluating species elevational distributions and habitat fragmentation to translate field data into actionable conservation policies (e.g., *Rana* trade management).

---

## 🌿 Fieldwork & Methodology

My work bridges fundamental field biology with applied conservation science. I regularly conduct field surveys across diverse Korean landscapes—from high-altitude mountainous streams inhabited by stream salamanders to rapidly expanding urban coastal zones.
