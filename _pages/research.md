My research integrates field ecology, physiological experiments, and policy design to address the global amphibian crisis. Here are my main research pillars:

---

### 🌊 1. Salinity Tolerance & Local Adaptation (The Sea Frog Project)
Amphibians are traditionally considered highly sensitive to salt due to their permeable skin. However, I investigated coastal populations of the Japanese tree frog (*Dryophytes japonicus*) breeding in saline rock pools in Jeju Island.
* **Key Focus:** Documenting the salinity threshold of tadpoles and looking at how micro-evolutionary adaptation allows them to thrive in coastal ecotones.
* **Significance:** Provides crucial insights into how coastal species might survive predicted sea-level rises caused by climate change.

---

### 🌾 2. Threatened Species & Agricultural Ecosystems
In South Korea, many amphibians depend heavily on traditional rice paddies. I study how modern agricultural practices, land reclamation, and changes in irrigation regimes affect the highly endangered Suweon tree frog (*Dryophytes suweonensis*).
* **Key Focus:** GIS-based mapping, habitat suitability modeling, and evaluating elevational distribution patterns across the Korean Peninsula.

---

### 🦎 3. Invasive Herpetofauna & Biosecurity Policies
Unregulated pet trade and global shipping introduce non-native species that threaten native biodiversity and act as vectors for diseases like chytridiomycosis.
* **Key Focus:** 
  * Tracking the establishment of the non-native Peking gecko (*Gekko swinhonis*) in Incheon.
  * Formulating actionable policy recommendations for the *Rana* frog trade in South Korea to prevent ecological and pathogen risks.
